# ASH Coop Bicycles Policy
This policy brings together various elements relating to bicycles, with the aim of improving the support offered to residents who use bicycles for transport and leisure activities, and need accessible and secure places to store them. The term ‘residents’ is used to reflect the fact that children, too, have bicycles.
This policy will be administered and developed by the Bicycles / Bikes Working Group. It is founded upon and informed by a prior consultation conducted with ASH Coop members via a survey (see 5. Excursus below).
There was clear consensus that members want the group to have the power to dispose of abandoned bikes; support the provision of safe and secure storage property by property as well as communally; and help ASH Coop become more self-sufficient in the matter of bike maintenance and repair.

## 1. Abandoned Bicycles
There appear to be many unused/abandoned bikes scattered around the grounds, including in existing bike shelters, adding to difficulties for storage of bikes in use, and for the use and maintenance of grounds. As a solution, ASH Coop will:

a. Periodically conduct a survey of bicycles left unused around the premises (including in any dedicated bike parking facilities), giving residents a month in which to identify bikes belonging to them that they want to keep.
b. After this consultation period, abandoned bikes will be removed and either renovated for further use or donated to charity.
c. Where possible, maintain a small number of formerly abandoned and renovated bicycles for general community use.

## 2. Bicycle Parking
Current provision for bicycle parking does not meet residents’ needs. As a result, bicycles are often kept in inconvenient places, such as inside shared houses or blocking access on Coop paths. There have increasingly been bicycle thefts from Coop property.
To improve this situation, ASH Coop will:

A. Over time, provide one secure bicycle parking space per resident – regardless of age – in the form of secure shelters for each house and/or communal shelters. Each resident will have one allocated, numbered place that can only be used for bicycle parking, transferable to the next resident in their property should they leave ASH Coop.
B. In consultation with residents, review, renovate, and update the existing shelters at either end of the property to make them more secure and accessible.
C. Consult and work with each residence requesting a shelter – on a first-come-first-served basis – to provide bike shelters of their chosen design and location adjacent to their property when practically and financially possible. Oversized/unusual machines will be approached on a case-by-case basis.

 D. Residents who wish to own more than one bicycle will make their own arrangements for storage of additional machines with the support of the Bicycles Working Group.

## 3. Cycle Maintenance & Repair
Bicycles are an accessible form of transport that can be simply maintained by anyone with access to appropriate tools, premises, and skills. However, those can be difficult to use in already-crowded homes. Therefore, ASH Coop will:

A. Develop provision for bicycle maintenance and repair through the purchase of communal specialist tools.
B. Consider the provision of a dedicated bicycle workshop.
C. Consult and collaborate with other relevant groups to initiate in-house and/or external training provision on bike repair and maintenance.
4. Disclaimer: Legal Liability
While ASH Coop will work with residents to ensure a secure bicycle space for each, it is not liable for compensation should a bicycle subsequently be stolen or damaged on site.
